急性和慢性肾脏疾病（CKD）是伴侣动物中的主要临床挑战，但用于逆转已确立损伤的治疗选择仍然有限。间充质干细胞（MSC）（MSCs）因其免疫调节和组织修复特性而成为一种有前景的治疗方法。本综述综合了关于猫犬肾脏疾病MSC治疗的现有证据，重点关注作用机制、预处理策略、给药途径及临床结果。主要发现表明，MSCs通过旁分泌介导的免疫调节而非直接分化发挥肾保护作用。静脉给药操作简单，但会导致>80%的细胞滞留于肺部，肾脏归巢不到5%；动脉或局部注射可将归巢率提高到15–20%，但存在操作风险且缺乏标准化剂量。
Acute and chronic kidney diseases are major clinical challenges in companion animals, yet therapeutic options for reversing established injury remain limited. Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) have emerged as a promising therapy due to their immunomodulatory and tissue-repair properties. This review synthesizes current evidence on MSC therapy for kidney disease in cats and dogs, with a focus on mechanisms, preconditioning strategies, delivery routes, and clinical outcomes. Key findings indicate that MSCs exert renoprotective effects primar - ily through paracrine-mediated immunomodulation rather than direct differ - entiation. Intravenous administration, while simple, results in >80% pulmonary entrapment and renal homing below 5%; arterial or local injection increases homing to 15–20% but carries procedural risks and lacks standardized dosing.
预处理策略（低氧、褪黑素、全反式维甲酸）可增强MSC在啮齿类动物模型中的存活和归巢，但针对猫和犬的特异性验证仍然有限。来自19项猫/狗研究的临床数据显示，MSC治疗总体安全，大多数不良事件与输注相关且为暂时性。在猫慢性肾病（CKD）中，虽然很少达到统计学显著性，但报告中普遍显示肾小球滤过率（GFR）和生活质量的改善趋势。在犬急性肾损伤中，MSC治疗可改善存活率和肾功能，但结果异质性较大，且主要来自实验诱导模型。主要证据缺口包括剂量缺乏标准化、MSC表征可变、随访时间短，以及长期安全性数据有限。未来，建立关于基于体重的剂量、MSC表征标准的共识，并在自然发生疾病中开展多中心随机对照试验，对于将前临床的潜力转化为临床应用至关重要。
Preconditioning strategies (hypoxia, melatonin, ATRA) enhance MSC survival and homing in rodent models, but felineand canine-specific validation remains limited. Clinical data from 19 studies in cats/dogs demonstrate that MSC therapy is generally safe, with most adverse events being infusion-related and transient. In feline CKD, consistent trends toward improved glomerular filtration rate and quality of life are reported, although statistical significance is rarely achieved. In canine AKI, MSC therapy improves survival and renal function, but results are heterogeneous and predominantly derived from experimentally induced models. Major evidence gaps include lack of dose standardization, variable MSC characterization, short follow-up durations, and limited long-term safety data. Moving forward, establishing consensus on weight-based dosing, MSC characterization standards, and multicenter randomized controlled trials in naturally occurring disease is essential to translate preclinical promise into clinical practice.

Spontaneous kidney disease is highly prevalent in companion animals, particularly cats and dogs, and closely mimics the pathological features observed in human kidney disorders (4, 5).
