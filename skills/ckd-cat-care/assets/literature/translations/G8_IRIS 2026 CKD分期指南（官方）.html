1. Staging of Chronic Kidney Disease based on blood creatinine and SDMA concentrations
在确诊慢性肾脏疾病（CKD）后，需要进行分期，以便为犬猫患者提供适当的治疗和监测。分期最初基于空腹血肌酐或空腹血 SDMA 浓度，或者（更好地）两者同时测定，并且在至少两次测定的水化、稳定患者中进行。然后，根据蛋白尿和血压对狗或猫进行亚分期。使用这些标准，可以针对这些病例提出一些经验性建议，关于使用哪种类型的治疗是合理的。此外，还可以根据临床经验对治疗的可能反应进行预测。
Staging is undertaken following diagnosis of chronic kidney disease (CKD) to facilitate appropriate treatment and monitoring of the canine or feline patient. Staging is based initially on fasting blood creatinine or fasting blood SDMA concentration or (preferably) both assessed on at least two occasions in a hydrated, stable patient. The dog or cat is then substaged based on proteinuria and blood pressure. Using these criteria, some empirical recommendations can be made about the type of treatment it would be logical to use for these cases. In addition, predictions based on clinical experience might be made about the likely response to treatment.

Substaging by Proteinuria The goal is to identify renal proteinuria having ruled out post-renal and pre-renal causes. Standard urine dipsticks can be insensitive therefore practitioners should consider using a more reliable quantitative test such as the urine protein to creatinine ratio (UP/C) or a species-specific albuminuria assay. The UP/C should be measured in all dogs and cats with CKD, provided there is no evidence of urinary tract inflammation or hemorrhage, and the routine measurement of plasma proteins has ruled out dysproteinemias. Ideally substaging should be done on the basis of at least two urine samples collected over a period of at least 2 weeks.
持续处于边缘蛋白尿状态的犬和猫患者应在2个月内重新评估，并根据情况重新分类。边缘蛋白尿在预测未来肾脏健康方面的意义尚未完全明确。IRIS的建议是继续监测犬的这种蛋白尿水平。考虑到与进行性肾脏疾病的关联，兽医可能会为持续边缘蛋白尿的猫提供治疗（见治疗指南）。对于任何用于降低肾小球高血压、滤过压力和蛋白尿的治疗，应通过UP/C在间隔内监测其反应。
Canine and feline patients that are persistently borderline proteinuric should be re-evaluated within 2 months and re-classified as appropriate. The significance of borderline proteinuria in predicting future renal health is not completely understood. IRIS’s recommendation is to continue to monitor this level of proteinuria in dogs. Veterinarians might offer treatment for cats with persistent borderline proteinuria considering the association with progressive kidney disease (see treatment guidelines). Response to any treatment given to reduce glomerular hypertension, filtration pressure, and proteinuria, should be monitored at intervals using UP/C.
